## 3. What “court of record” and “public record” mean here

### 3.1 Court of record

A **court of record** is a court whose proceedings and records are maintained in the manner required by law. The description characterises the court and its official record. It does **not** certify that every statement in every filed annexure is true, admissible, adopted or judicially endorsed.

Under section **31** of the **Superior Courts Act 10 of 2013**, every Superior Court is a court of record. Under section **4** of the **Magistrates’ Courts Act 32 of 1944**, every Magistrate’s Court is a court of record. These provisions do not turn a private document into a judicial finding merely because it is filed.

### 3.2 Public access is real but qualified

The following rules must be read together rather than replaced by an unlimited “public means online” claim:

- Section **32** of the Superior Courts Act provides for public proceedings, subject to legislation and special judicial directions.
- Section **5** of the Magistrates’ Courts Act provides for open and recorded proceedings, subject to statutory exceptions and judicial directions.
- Section **7** of the Magistrates’ Courts Act provides public access to court records **under supervision of the clerk of the court, at convenient times and upon prescribed fees**, subject to court rules, section 7A and specified statutory restrictions.
- Section **32** of the Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996 establishes the constitutional right of access to information, with national legislation giving effect to it and permitting reasonable measures to alleviate administrative and financial burdens.
- The **Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 (PAIA)** is part of that access framework; it is not a blanket rule that every court document must be freely downloadable. In particular, PAIA contains exclusions and limitations, including section **7** concerning records requested for criminal or civil proceedings after those proceedings have commenced where access for that purpose is provided for in another law. Court-specific legislation, rules and lawful directions remain relevant.

Accordingly, this repository must not state that all filed material is inspectable by anyone at any time, that every record is available online, or that filing eliminates privacy, confidentiality, restriction or access procedures. Those propositions were not established by this review.

## 4. The status of Constitution v8.0

For repository and product purposes, Constitution v8.0 is a **private governance instrument**. Its presence in a court filing can establish provenance for the fact that a copy was filed, subject to verification of the court copy. It does not, by itself:

- enact legislation or create jurisdiction;
- bind a court, organ of state, government or unrelated person;
- bind unrelated commercial, open-source, sovereign or other AI systems;
- make its assertions judicial findings;
- make the platform “constitutional law” in the public-law sense; or
- establish admissibility, reliability, accuracy or courtroom acceptance.

The repository’s locked Constitution copies are not edited by this note. Any contradiction between promotional wording and the limits recorded here must be resolved through the repository’s controlled review process, not by silently altering a locked instrument.

## 5. Seals, hashes and timestamps are not judicial validation

A cryptographic seal is **tamper-evident, not immutable**. A SHA-512 digest can support detection of changed bytes relative to a recorded digest. An OpenTimestamps proof, once independently verified, supports the conclusion that the relevant hash existed no later than the confirming Bitcoin block. Neither mechanism, alone or together, proves:

- who authored or supplied the source document;
- that its factual assertions are true;
- that a court admitted or accepted it;
- that the analysis is complete or accurate;
- that a court adopted the methodology; or
- that a copy was not altered and re-sealed with a different valid seal.

Court-record status, source integrity, authorship, truth, admissibility and judicial acceptance are separate questions and must remain separate in code, reports, prompts and public documentation.
